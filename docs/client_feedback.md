# Client Feedback and Redesign Brief
## Maine Makers Workshop

**Website under review:** https://husson-webdesign.github.io/statnic-P8-MainMakers/

Hello,

Thank you for sharing the first version of our website. I appreciate that you have already created separate pages for our story, workshops, memberships, and contact information. The workshop descriptions include helpful details such as skill level, price, date, duration, and materials. I also like that the site explains that membership does not automatically authorize visitors to use every tool, and that the workshop page offers a way to sort by price.

For the next version, I would like the website to feel more like a creative, active makerspace. Visitors should be able to picture what they can make, find a workshop suited to their interests, compare membership choices, and understand how to get started. The current pages contain a great deal of useful information, but much of it is presented as long blocks of text and lists. The homepage in particular feels sparse and does not yet show the excitement of learning and making together.

## Phase 1: Strengthen Our Identity and Message

### 1. Create a memorable makerspace identity

The dark blue, cream, and gold colors provide a starting point. Please develop them into a more distinctive and consistent identity that feels creative, practical, welcoming, and suitable for beginners as well as experienced makers.

I would like a polished wordmark or simple logo, a coordinated color palette, readable typography, and consistent styles for buttons, cards, headings, and page sections. Our tagline, **“Learn it. Make it. Build something new.”**, is a good message to build on.

### 2. Make the homepage more inviting

The current homepage gives a thorough introduction, but the long paragraph and large empty area do not help visitors picture the experience. Please create a stronger opening message and break the introduction into shorter, easier-to-read sections.

Help visitors understand that we offer hands-on workshops and shared workspace. Give them clear choices such as **Explore Workshops** and **Compare Memberships**. If our exact location and service details are confirmed in the project brief, make them easy to find; please ask me rather than guessing.

### 3. Tell our story and introduce our people

The About page already explains learning by making and our commitment to safety. Please build on that foundation with a more personal presentation of our community and how newcomers can feel comfortable joining.

The Contact page contains introductions to Julia, Ben, Priya, and Megan. Consider presenting the approved staff information in a welcoming team section on the About page, with a clear link to contact us. Do not invent qualifications, staff photographs, awards, or business history.

## Phase 2: Improve the Page Layout and Visual Design

### 4. Make the homepage a useful overview

Please add short sections introducing our workshop categories, the membership options, and the steps a new visitor can take to get started. Feature a few approved workshops and link to the complete list.

A visitor should be able to understand the difference between taking a workshop and using shared workspace without reading every page first.

### 5. Show the experience of making

Please use images that help visitors picture woodworking, crafts, electronics, and 3D printing. Show people learning or working safely where appropriate, using images you have permission to use. Document their sources and provide useful descriptions.

If a photograph does not show our actual space, staff, or projects, do not present it as such. Do not invent student testimonials or completed project results.

### 6. Make long pages easier to scan

The Workshops, Memberships, and Contact pages contain valuable details, but some sections are lengthy. Use a consistent content width, headings, spacing, cards, and summaries so visitors can find what matters. Keep full safety and policy details available rather than hiding or removing them.

Make workshop dates, skill levels, durations, and prices easy to distinguish. Use a layout that remains comfortable on small screens.

### 7. Create a useful footer

The current footer contains a copyright line. Please add approved contact and location details, operating hours, and links to the main pages or important safety information. Keep the footer organized and consistent across the website.

## Phase 3: Improve Workshop Discovery

### 8. Make workshop choices easier to compare

The six current workshop descriptions are a strong foundation. Please present each workshop consistently with its name, category, skill level, date, duration, price, materials information, and a short description. Let visitors open or read the complete details when they need them.

Make it easy for a visitor to go from a workshop to a demonstration inquiry about that specific class. Where a workshop has prerequisites, age restrictions, or protective equipment requirements, show the approved information clearly. Ask me about missing requirements rather than inventing them.

### 9. Clarify dates and availability

The six dates currently shown run from **September 19 through October 24, 2026**, so they are past dates as of this review. Each workshop also shows a number of available seats. Please confirm whether these are sample listings or if new schedules have been provided.

For the class project, label sample dates and seat counts clearly. Do not imply that availability is live or that a past workshop is open for registration. Ask me for approved future dates rather than making them up.

### 10. Build on the existing sort control

I appreciate the existing sort-by-price choice. Please retain it and test that it orders the displayed workshops correctly. Add an easy way to find workshops by category or skill level, and let visitors clear their choices and see all workshops again.

If no results match, show a friendly message. Clearly indicate whether displayed dates and availability are sample information. Do not imply that filtering reserves a place.

## Phase 4: Make Memberships Clear and Trustworthy

### 11. Help visitors compare the plans

The Day Pass, Monthly Maker, and Unlimited Maker options already include prices and useful explanations. Please make them easier to compare at a glance, while retaining the full policies and the important distinction between workspace access and permission to use restricted equipment.

Show approved prices, the access period, key benefits, and relevant limitations consistently. Make it clear that “Unlimited” does not mean unrestricted equipment use. Confirm access hours, discounts, storage, prices, and other plan details against the project brief; do not create new benefits or promises.

### 12. Give membership visitors a clear next step

Add an inquiry option for each plan. A visitor should be able to prepare a question about a selected membership, change their choice, or ask a general question. Do not imply that clicking the button purchases a membership or grants access.

## Phase 5: Safety, Contact, and Customer Journey

### 13. Keep safety information visible and easy to find

I appreciate the detailed safety rules and the explanation that some equipment requires orientation, training, supervision, or approval. Please keep those details prominent and organize them so newcomers can understand what applies before participating.

Link to the relevant safety information from workshop and membership pages. Confirm any activity-specific rules and age requirements with me; do not invent equipment permissions, safety guarantees, or training policies.

### 14. Organize the Contact page around visitor needs

The Contact page currently includes the form, hours, staff information, safety rules, and a long FAQ. Please make it easier to find the most important visiting and inquiry information. Consider moving the team introductions to About and placing safety or FAQ material in dedicated sections or pages with clear links.

The listed `.example` email address and `555` phone number appear to be sample details. Please confirm the approved contact information, operating hours, and location against the project brief and identify fictional details as samples. Do not invent an address or a map location.

### 15. Make the form an honest demonstration

The current form says **“Send Message”** and asks for a workshop and experience level. Because this class project uses GitHub Pages, I understand that the form will not send or save information. Please place a clear demonstration notice before the form and rename the button to something accurate, such as **“Preview Inquiry.”**

Provide helpful guidance for missing information. After a visitor completes the form, show a readable preview and allow them to make changes. Do not say that a message was sent, a seat reserved, a registration completed, or a membership purchased.

Allow visitors to prepare either a workshop inquiry, membership inquiry, or general question. If they arrive from a specific workshop or membership, preselect that choice where possible and let them change it. Do not request more personal information than the demonstration needs.

## Phase 6: Organize Information for Future Updates

### 16. Maintain workshop details in JSON

I would like future updates to workshop descriptions, prices, dates, and categories to be easier for a developer to manage. Please keep the approved workshop information in a separate **JSON file** and use it to display the workshops on the website.

Please make sure:

- All six approved workshop descriptions are included.
- Each entry consistently contains its name, category, skill level, description, duration, approved price, and any approved date and materials information.
- Sample dates and seat counts are identified clearly and are not presented as live availability.
- Sorting and filtering use the same information shown on the page.
- Visitors see a helpful message if workshop information cannot be displayed.
- Project documentation explains where a future developer can find and update the information.

If time permits, organize the approved membership options in a separate JSON file as well. Do not invent new dates, prices, policies, or benefits.

## Phase 7: Navigation, Mobile Use, and Accessibility

### 17. Make navigation consistent and useful

Keep the existing separate pages and make the current page clear. The business name or logo should return visitors home. Add clear routes between workshops, memberships, safety information, and the inquiry demonstration.

On a phone, the navigation should be easy to open and close if a compact menu is needed. Test the menu with a keyboard as well as a touchscreen.

### 18. Make every page comfortable to use

Please test desktop, tablet, and phone sizes. Text should be readable without zooming; cards, images, and forms should fit the screen; and visitors should not need to scroll sideways.

Make sure visitors can navigate with a keyboard, see which item has focus, understand form labels and error messages, and read text against its background. Workshop sorting and filters should work without a mouse. Give pages clear, meaningful titles and organize headings so visitors can understand the content.

## Phase 8: Final Review and Handoff

### 19. Test the published website

Before presenting the redesign, please check:

- Every page opens directly and can be reached through navigation.
- Images, links, buttons, workshop sorting, and filters work.
- Workshop dates and seat counts are accurately labeled as samples where applicable.
- Workshop and membership descriptions, prices, and safety details match approved information.
- The demonstration form does not claim to send information or reserve a place.
- JSON-powered workshop information loads on GitHub Pages.
- Pages work on phone, tablet, and desktop sizes.
- Keyboard navigation, focus visibility, readability, and image descriptions are reviewed.
- Spelling is checked, including “workshop organizatio” in the Introduction to Woodworking Tools description.
- Class-project wording such as “Students may explain tool access” is rewritten for visitors while keeping the meaning accurate.

### 20. Provide a professional handoff

Please provide the published website link, repository link, a brief explanation of the redesign, desktop and phone screenshots, image sources, known limitations, and a list of details needing my confirmation. Include instructions explaining where a future developer can update workshops, memberships, and sample dates.
